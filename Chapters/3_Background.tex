\section{BACKGROUND}
\subsection{Project Context}
The [Project Name] project prioritized individual wellbeing outcomes through inclusive-participatory education. While collaboration was a pedagogical tool, the goal was empowering individuals to create their own content independently using personal devices. The workshops aimed to build confidence, creative agency, and technical skills for autonomous video creation, From April–July 2025, we ran 7 structured workshops combining technical skill-building with creative expression. 
The workshops were co-designed and delivered with three UK BVI-sector partners (two in [City 1], one in [City 2]), here anonymized as [Partner A], [Partner B], and [Partner C], who supported recruitment, co-facilitation, and integration within existing community programmes. 

\subsection{Workshop Structure and Progression}
We delivered seven workshops across two sites using a hybrid format. [City 2] (as a rural site) cohort preferring online sessions and the [City 1] cohort favouring in-person meetings, provided valuable comparative data on delivery modes. The [City 1] cohort attended three in-person sessions and one online session; the [City 2] cohort completed three online sessions. Each workshop ran 3–4 hours, balancing hands-on practice with participant energy and attention.

Each session opened with a brief warm-up: introductions (with visual descriptions), aims for the day, and an outline of activities. Core teaching combined short demonstrations with guided, hands-on practice. In person, a teaching team of two tutors and one facilitator circulated to provide just-in-time assistance; online sessions were tutor-led with support provided remotely. Sessions closed with a 30-minute group reflection, focusing on challenges, accessibility, and next steps. Each workshop was supported by accessible workbooks providing step-by-step instructions and teaching slides adapted for screen reader compatibility (See Appendix).

The curriculum followed a scaffolded path (see \autoref{fig:workshhop}):
\begin{itemize}
    \item Foundation phase: Orientation to accessible apps; basic equipment (ring lights, tripods, microphones); fundamentals of audio/video capture. Emphasis on building confidence with camera pointing, framing via non-visual feedback, and creating simple recordings with music.
    \item Skill-development phase: Shot composition (close/wide), trimming, basic editing, and music integration. Focus on spatial awareness and sequencing without relying solely on vision.  Seeing AI served as a critical tool for framing and recording, providing real-time audio feedback about visual composition.
    \item Advanced techniques: Voice-over narration and exploration of AI-assisted tools (e.g., generating simple graphics such as stylized selfies, logos, and text assets) to expand creative options.
    \item Showcase \& dissemination: Peer sharing of works-in-progress, advanced prompting strategies, and approaches to online sharing and promotion to sustain community support.
\end{itemize}
At [City 1], each phase mapped to a single workshop. At [City 2], Foundation and Skill-development were compressed into one workshop; Advanced techniques were the focus of the second; the third emphasized Showcase \& dissemination.

We engaged 13 BVI participants across two cohorts; three withdrew during the series  due to time conflicts or body conditions, are not included in the analysis. Recruitment used purposive sampling through partner networks to support diversity in vision conditions, technology experience, and creative backgrounds. Inclusion criteria were self-identification as blind or visually impaired and basic smartphone familiarity; prior video experience was not required. The analytic sample comprises 10 participants (see \autoref{tab:demo}, IDs use P for participants and T for teaching team): 8 women and 2 men; ages 26–39 (n=4), 40–65 (n=5), and over 65 (n=1). Vision profiles were evenly split between low vision (n=5) and blind (n=5). Self-rated media-creation experience on a 0–5 scale averaged 1.5 (median 1, range 1–3). 2 participants (P3, P4) attended with sighted guides.

The facilitation team comprised four members aged 20-46 with gender balance (2 women, 2 men) and substantial media-creation experience (mean 4.0, range 3-5): a sighted lead tutor (T1), two BVI tutors with video expertise—one low vision (T3) and one blind (T4)—and one sighted facilitator providing technical support and visual description (T2). The team included one tutor from the research team and one tutor-trainee from the partner community (TPT), supporting capacity building within the BVI community.
