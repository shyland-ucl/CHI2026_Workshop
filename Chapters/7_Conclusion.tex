\section{CONCLUSION}
Across seven workshops with 10 BVI participants in two UK sites, we examined how ability-diverse teams enact access in video creation. Combining multimodal conversation analysis with reflexive thematic analysis, we identified three interlocking asymmetries (expertise, tool ecosystem, interaction paradigm) and four collaborative patterns through which participants navigated them: direct haptic mediation, cross-modal translation, task-specific delegation, and peer-to-peer assistance. Delegation in particular holds an internal tension between agentic hand-off and pre-emptive sighted-grab.

We proposed dynamic standard-making as a generative reframing of access: the situated co-construction of temporary interactional standards between specific collaborators. Rather than contesting Universal Design for Learning, dynamic standard-making complements it by accounting for what cannot be designed in advance. Stronger universal-design provision reduces the volume of standard-making required, but some will always remain because no design can anticipate every collaborator and every task.

The findings carry implications for accessible video tool design, inclusive curriculum design, and the broader study of access --- grounded in the specific patterns we observed, and offered as a starting point for further work on how access is collectively achieved in ability-diverse settings.
